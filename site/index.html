02 · Case study · in production use

NCI RIMS — a compliance operating system for a pharmaceutical manufacturer.

Built in-house at NCI Pharm Chem. Ind., Lagos, where I am the Regulatory Officer. Every screenshot below is a demonstration tenant carrying invented products — the real register never leaves the company, which is rather the point of the work. Everything else is exactly what was built and what it does.

The problem

Over a hundred NAFDAC-regulated products were being tracked in spreadsheets. Registrations lapsed quietly because nothing watched the dates. Letters to the agency were re-typed by hand for every trip. Nobody could prove who changed what, or when — which is the one question an inspection actually asks.

What I built

  • Expiry radar. Every registration is watched; renewals are raised 90+ days before the deadline instead of being noticed after it.
  • Letter studio. Agency correspondence generated from the register, with QR-verifiable signatures, on a letterhead that prints to the millimetre.
  • Controlled document vault. Versioned files with role-based approvals, so the current copy is never in doubt.
  • Append-only audit trail. Over 50,000 events recorded and never editable — 21 CFR Part 11 supportable in shape.
  • Port-clearance countdowns. Demurrage clocks on arriving consignments, because that delay is billed by the day.
  • Role-based access. Server-resolved permissions; the browser never decides what a user may see.

How it is kept honest

Every figure the system prints is traceable to the record it came from. Reports state their own coverage — if 14 products have no expiry date on file, the report says so rather than quietly averaging them away. Print output is verified as real A4 PDFs, not screenshots.
