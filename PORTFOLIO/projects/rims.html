NCI RIMS — the Regulatory Inventory
Management System

A compliance backbone for a NAFDAC-regulated pharmaceutical manufacturer: one source of truth for products, registrations, documents, letters, port clearances and deadlines — designed print-first, secured role-by-role, and honest about what it is and isn't yet.

RoleSole designer & builder

DeployedVercel · PWA · mobile-first MD
StatusIn production use · private
DEMO REEL · recorded live on the production system — executive dashboard with renewal calendar → product catalogue → Regulatory Academy → letter studio

The problem

Fifty-plus regulated products, each with a registration that expires, a dossier that must stay current, artwork that must match approvals, and permits with hard statutory dates. All of it lived in spreadsheets, folders and institutional memory. A missed renewal date is not an inconvenience in this industry — it is a product off the market. Meanwhile the managing director reads everything on a phone, and regulators expect documents that look like they came from a disciplined quality system.

What I built

Fourteen modules behind one role-aware sidebar, each earned its place by killing a real failure mode:

Executive dashboard KPIs, renewal pressure, port exposure — and a one-tap executive report in the house print design.
Expiry radar Every registration scored by days-left bands; print dossier per product, A4-exact.
Product portfolio & catalogue Data-derived artwork panels and photo cards — never guessed labels.
Document vault Versions, approval states, ownership; 600+ controlled records migrated in.
Letter studio Template-driven official letters with stored signatures and QR verification links.
NAFDAC checklists Requirement lists rebuilt from official guidelines; one-page A4 print sheets with Reviewed-by signature blocks.
Port clearance board Consignment timelines with free-day and demurrage countdowns.
Evidence packs Audit-ready bundles assembled per product in one click.
Monday briefing The week's regulatory picture, auto-written from live data.
Regulatory Academy Internal training notes, admin-authored.
AI assistant RAG over the company's own documents — answers labelled guidance-only, never invents product facts.
RBAC + audit trail Role matrix per page and action; append-only audit events on every write.
Scan & fill / offline PWA Field capture on poor networks; installs to the MD's phone home screen.
Print design system One A4 grammar for checklists, dossiers, certificates and reports — signature blocks included.

Engineering decisions I'd defend in a review

  • Security at the database, not the page. Postgres row-level security plus a role matrix; privileged writes only through service-role server actions behind capability checks.
  • Print as a first-class output. Screen/print twin stylesheets so what the MD sees is exactly what the printer produces — one A4 page for checklists, landscape certificates, 4-page executive reports.
  • Verification over vibes. Playwright end-to-end probes against the deployed production build after every release; database state re-checked through the API, not just screenshots.
  • Data honesty rules. No document is ever labelled to a product by assumption; empty beats wrong; every generated figure carries its source and generation date.
  • Migration without drama. 600+ legacy records reconciled and imported with duplicate handling and rollback-safe batches.
Honesty guard-rail, by design: RIMS is described as GxP-aware and 21 CFR Part 11 supportable — e-signature fields, audit trail and access control are in place, but the word "validated" will not appear anywhere until penetration testing (GOL-01) and staff training (GOL-03) are complete. AI features answer as guidance only. A regulated system that overclaims is a liability wearing a lab coat.

Outcomes so far

  • Renewals now surface 90+ days before expiry with the packet list already assembled.
  • 621 controlled documents under version and approval control, searchable in one vault.
  • Official correspondence drafted, signed and QR-verified from one studio — minutes instead of days.
  • The MD runs the business from a phone: mobile-collapsed navigation, offline installs, one-tap reports.

What I learned

Regulated industries don't need more software; they need evidence machines. Every feature that survived contact with the business was one that produced a defensible artifact — a sheet, a pack, a trail. The rest was decoration, and I deleted it.
